The Chernarus Archive

The Story
of Chernarus

Nobody agrees on exactly how it started. Some blame the virus. Others blame the experiments. The old survivors whisper about government bunkers, buried laboratories and cargo ships that should have never made landfall.

Whatever the truth is, it does not matter anymore. The world ended—and Chernarus was left to burn.

The Ashfall

When civilization collapsed, the infected consumed the cities first.

The military fought back. Then the military disappeared. Aircraft fell from the sky. Ships drifted empty along the coast. Entire towns vanished beneath smoke and silence.

For years the land sat abandoned, rotting beneath endless storms and the footsteps of the dead. Then something changed.

The fires never truly went out. Deep beneath the ruined world, old systems continued running. Factories hummed. Generators churned. Secret facilities remained powered long after their creators died.

And somewhere in the darkness, someone was still pulling strings.

The Ember Research Institute

The secrets of Project Remnant are still waiting to be uncovered.

Long before the world fell silent, the classified Ember Research Institute operated beneath the military installations of Chernarus. Officially, it never existed. Unofficially, the fragments left behind tell a far darker story.

Scientists pursued experimental treatments, biological adaptation and a classified initiative known only as Project Remnant. Their work was distributed through military checkpoints, underground laboratories and fortified containment sites.

Then the facilities went dark. Communications ceased. Emergency lockdowns were triggered. The evacuation record is missing, and the transmissions that remain end without an answer.

Some Institute personnel are presumed lost. Other evidence suggests that parts of the organization may have survived the collapse—or that someone else learned to operate what they left behind. Contradictory reports have kept the question open: who, if anyone, still controls the locked doors?

The Distributed SAFESTORE Network

The coloured buildings were never ordinary storerooms.

The coloured SAFESTORE buildings were the Ember Research Institute’s distributed supply depots: a network designed to keep critical materials moving even when the main facilities were cut off.

Different clearance levels supported different kinds of work. Some depots protected civilian survival supplies. Others supported field operations. The highest clearances held increasingly specialized research materials and equipment.

That old logic still shapes the ruins. A coloured door is not just a locked room; it is a surviving piece of the Institute’s supply chain. The network recognizes clearance levels even when the people who created them are gone.

Reconstructed Credentials

Access is rebuilt piece by piece.

Survivors do not simply stumble across a drawer full of working coloured DNA cards. Scavengers recover packaged chips, blank card bodies and electronic components from the old economy, then assemble the tools needed to make sense of them.

With a workbench, patience and the right parts, a survivor can reconstruct an access credential from fragments that were never meant to be separated. The process is less like finding a key and more like rebuilding a language the old security network still understands.

The practical steps, material requirements and card-repair rules belong in the DNA Keycards & Strongrooms guide. The lore only gives the larger picture: every working credential is evidence that the old system can still be made to recognize a new intruder.

Red Rooms and Restricted Research

The top SAFESTORE clearance points toward deeper doors.

A Red DNA card opens the Red SAFESTORE. It does not open every facility beyond it.

Red-room rewards may include recovered credentials for separate restricted research facilities. Those credentials are not guaranteed, and each one belongs to a matching destination. A credential for Fort Greenville is not a credential for Fort Kamensk, Tower Hill or Fort Tisy.

The progression is deliberate: Red DNA grants access to the Red depot, while a recovered facility credential grants access to the facility named by its own clearance. The Locked Research Facilities guide covers those destinations and their player-facing risks without pretending the Institute’s final secrets are already known.

The Ember Command Annex

Emergency broadcasts still break through the silence.

Following the collapse, surviving staff established the Ember Command Annex to coordinate emergency broadcasts, recovery efforts and supply deployments.

The Annex may be abandoned, damaged or still listening. No surviving record settles the matter. Every transmission that reaches the surface raises the same question: is it an old system repeating its last orders, or is someone still choosing what the world hears?

The ATF Response

Every breach tells someone that an intruder has arrived.

An organized armed force known as the ATF responds to certain security breaches and attempts to recover restricted property. Opening a DNA crate or unlocking a research facility can bring reinforcements who pursue the survivor responsible.

No reliable record explains exactly how the ATF is connected to the Institute. They may be remnants of an older security structure, a force operating from the same instructions, or something more recent wearing the old system’s authority. The evidence is too incomplete to make that relationship canon.

What is certain is the response: a door opening can become a hunt. The DNA guide’s ATF warning and the research-facilities guide explain the current gameplay mechanics. The lore keeps the larger question unanswered.

The Ashguard

Ashguard has its own name, its own reach and its own unanswered questions.

The Ashguard is a distinct presence in Chernarus, moving through the same broken world as the settlements, traders and scavenger crews. They are not simply another label for the ATF, and no confirmed record places them on either side of the Institute’s old security network.

Their use of equipment resembling ATF equipment has encouraged rumors of diverted supplies, shared caches or an undisclosed connection. Rumors are not proof. For now, Ashguard’s relationship to the ATF remains an open question—one more thread survivors can follow without knowing where it ends.

The Rise of the Cinder Market

When governments die, traders thrive.

As settlements formed across the wasteland, a shadow economy emerged. Smugglers moved contraband through hidden channels. Mercenaries guarded shipments. Scavengers risked their lives retrieving technology from infected zones.

Eventually, these operations united under a single network: the Cinder Market. Located far from the eyes of ordinary survivors, it deals in weapons, technology, contraband and artifacts—if you have enough courage, they will buy almost anything.

The Rustwake Marina

The sea keeps its secrets.

As roads became more dangerous, sailors reclaimed the coastline. Abandoned fishing vessels were repaired, cargo boats were salvaged and new routes opened across the sea.

What began as a collection of scavengers became Rustwake Marina, the primary hub for maritime travel throughout the region. Some captains make fortunes moving supplies. Others disappear without explanation.

The Deep Vein

Some treasure lies beneath the earth.

Across Chernarus, rumors follow a weathered prospector known simply as Lewis of The Deep Vein. No one knows where he came from or where he goes when he leaves.

Whenever Lewis arrives, miners emerge carrying sacks of ore, gemstones and precious metals. He has little interest in weapons or technology. His wealth comes from the earth itself.

Some believe he worked the mines before the collapse. Others claim he possesses maps to deposits nobody else has discovered. Lewis never confirms either story. He weighs the ore, pays the price and moves on.

The Economy of the Ashes

In the new world, survival is wealth.

Cash remains the backbone of commerce throughout Chernarus. Traders accept it and settlements rely on it. Hunting, fishing, mining, scavenging, crafting and hard work can still build a living.

Zombie Notes are found in more than one kind of pocket. Infected can carry them, but armed patrols have been known to carry them too. Their value is recognized across the region, while their origin remains unexplained; no official account has survived to say who first issued them or why.

The uncertainty has not stopped survivors from using them. A note can still become a meal, a repair, a tool or a reason to take one more risk on the road.

The Machines That Remain

Not all threats are infected.

Across Chernarus, automated defenses continue operating years after their owners disappeared. Military patrols wander old routes. Security systems protect abandoned facilities. Unknown forces continue moving equipment and supplies through the region.

Some survivors believe remnants of the Ember Research Institute are still active. Others believe the machines are following instructions no living authority can explain. The ATF response, the sealed depots and the locked research sites all point to a network that still recognizes trespassers.

Survivors are rebuilding a home while discovering that the old security network still considers them intruders.

Life Beneath the Ashes

Survival is becoming preparation.

Not every survivor is a soldier. Some become fishermen. Some become hunters. Some establish trading companies. Others build fortified homesteads, workshops and small pieces of peace in an otherwise broken world.

Food preservation has become part of that rebuilding. A sealed jar or carefully prepared can is a practical promise that a community is preparing for the next season instead of surviving one meal at a time. The Canning & Food Preservation guide covers the working process; the meaning is visible in every stocked shelf and shared table.

Mining supplies the raw materials. Workshops turn scavenged parts into tools. Trade connects the people who grow, repair, hunt, build and explore. These are not signs that the old world has returned. They are signs that something new is being built.

The Truth

The world ended. Your story did not.

The world above may have burned. Civilization may have collapsed. The infected may own the roads. But humanity is not gone.

Trading. Building. Preserving. Exploring. Fighting. Surviving.

The ashes of the old world cover every city, road and ruined military base. Beneath them, communities are learning how to make food last, how to repair what they find and how to turn dangerous knowledge into another day of shelter.

The Institute’s doors still hold questions. The ATF still answers some of them with force. The Ashguard, the traders and the settlements all carry pieces of a history no one fully understands.

What comes next is up to you.
