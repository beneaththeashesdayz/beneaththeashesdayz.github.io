Electrical Systems

PowerGrid
Guide

Build, wire and control an electrical network in DayZ. This player guide explains the main PowerGrid components and what each one is used for.

Open PowerGrid Wiki →

Power Sources

  • Solar Panel: T1 produces 20 u/s and can be upgraded to T2 for 50 u/s. Output follows the day/night cycle.
  • Furnace: Burns items to generate 50 u/s.
  • Battery Adapter: Provides a cradle for vanilla car and truck batteries.

Energy Storage

  • Battery Medium: 10,000 u capacity.
  • Battery Large: 50,000 u capacity.
  • Batteries charge from surplus power and discharge to supplement the grid. LED indicators show their charge state.

Power Distribution

  • Splitter: One input to three outputs.
  • Combiner: Two inputs to one output.
  • Ceiling Light: Passthrough connection with overhead lighting.
  • Cable Reel: Tool used to connect components in the grid.

Switches & Control

  • Push Button: Momentary two-second pulse.
  • Switch V2: Latching lever toggle.
  • Remote Controls: Push Button Remote and Switch V2 Remote allow wireless RF control through an Intercom.
  • Remote Controller: Handheld device that pairs with RF switches.

Logic & Automation

  • AND, OR and XOR gates for circuit logic.
  • Memory Cell: Stateful latch with Toggle, Set and Reset inputs plus Normal and Inverted outputs.
  • Electronic Counter: Seven-segment display that counts pulses and outputs on overflow.
  • Motion Sensor, Pressure Pad and Laser Detector for detection-based circuits.

Devices & Appliances

  • CCTV System with Security Camera and Monitor.
  • Directional Searchlight with pitch and yaw control.
  • Water Pump T1/T2 with filter and optional 50L tank upgrade.
  • Fridge, Electric Stove, Sprinkler and Door Controller.
  • Intercom, Speaker, Sorter and BTC ATM.
Start simple: connect a power source to storage or a device, then add cable management, switches and logic as your network grows. The official wiki contains the detailed setup information for individual components.

Player Reference

PowerGrid adds everything from basic lighting and refrigeration to automated doors, security cameras, crop watering, sorting and remote-controlled systems. Build the network around what your group actually needs, then expand it as your base grows.
