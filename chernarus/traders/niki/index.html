Archive // Trader Dossier

Trader Lore

Niki keeps the vehicles of Chernarus moving, though nobody seems entirely sure where he learned to do it.

Before the collapse, scattered Ember Research Institute records reference a technician assigned to the Institute's motor pool and field recovery division. The name on several of those records has been damaged or deliberately removed. What remains is a personnel photograph bearing an uncomfortable resemblance to the man now working behind the counter at Scorched Isle Market.

Niki doesn't talk about Ember.

Ask him about an engine and he'll tell you exactly what's wrong before you've finished describing the noise. Ask him about the Institute and that friendly smile tends to linger just a little too long.

His workshop contains parts from vehicles that haven't been manufactured in years, components stamped with Ember inventory numbers, and occasionally something that doesn't appear to belong to a civilian vehicle at all.

Nobody has been brave enough to ask where he gets them.

Most survivors have decided it doesn't matter.

The stranger question is why so much of it is free. Niki will hand over batteries, radiators, body panels and repair parts with barely a second glance. He calls it keeping Chernarus moving. Others wonder whether the parts were ever his to sell in the first place.

Then there are the personal garages.

Niki sells access through small retrieval tokens, but he never actually calls them garages. The tokens appear to interface with remnants of an automated Ember vehicle storage and recovery network, a system once used to secure, rotate and relocate Institute assets across Chernarus. Somehow, decades after the world that built it disappeared, Niki can still make it answer.

Where the vehicles go while stored, how Niki retained access, and why the Institute designed the system in the first place are questions he has never volunteered to answer.

Survivors generally learn not to ask.

“The parts are free. Niki is helpful. It's probably better to leave it at that.”
