Archive // Trader Dossier

Frida of Emberline Parts

Frida can identify a vehicle from the shape of a door hinge, the spacing of a wheel stud or the irritated noise Seth makes when he realizes he has promised a buyer something difficult to replace.

Before Emberline had a showroom, it had Frida's inventory ledger.

Every recovery brought back more than a vehicle. Wrecks were stripped, panels were sorted by model and color, usable wheels were tagged, and keys were separated from the mountain of scrap that accumulated around the yard. Where other survivors saw a pile of mismatched metal, Frida saw the beginning of a supply network.

That network became Emberline Parts.

Seth handles the rare vehicles and the salesmanship. Frida handles everything required after the shine wears off. She knows which panels interchange, which color variant is actually the one a survivor is describing, and which wheel absolutely will not fit no matter how confidently somebody insists otherwise.

Most of the standard replacement parts cross her counter for little or nothing. Frida would rather keep an Emberline vehicle on the road than watch it abandoned because its owner could not replace a door. Keys are different. A working key represents access, ownership and one less vehicle disappearing into somebody else's hands, so those retain a price of their own.

Her counter is not a repair shop, and Frida will make that distinction very clear. She supplies the correct part. What happens between the counter and the vehicle is the survivor's responsibility.

If Seth sells the dream, Frida is the reason it survives first contact with Chernarus.
