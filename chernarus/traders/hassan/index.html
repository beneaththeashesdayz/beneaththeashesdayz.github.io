Hassan of Cinder Market

Before the outbreak, Hassan worked the unremarkable end of pharmaceutical distribution: manifests, sealed shipments, missing inventory and the quiet arithmetic that decided whether a delivery reached its destination. He learned early that the most valuable person in any supply chain was not the one who made the goods. It was the one who knew where everything had to go next.

When Chernarus collapsed, the warehouses emptied in days. The knowledge did not.

Hassan moved east with a ledger in his coat and a talent for finding the people who could still finish what they started. Growers brought him raw harvests. Chemists arrived with half-working stations. Smugglers brought promises. He paid none of them until the product was complete, properly packed and ready to move.

That rule built his reputation—and eventually his counter at Cinder Market. The sea keeps away casual customers, while the dangerous crossing ensures most visitors arrive with serious business. Hassan likes it that way.

His shelves hold more than finished goods. Drying racks, hydroponic systems, lab stations and production equipment move through his hands because every reliable producer becomes another reliable supplier. Hassan will sell survivors the tools, but he expects them to learn the work themselves.

He inspects every package, remembers every bad deal and never raises his voice when refusing one. Survivors who bring him clean, finished product are paid without ceremony. Those who waste his time are remembered just as efficiently.

At Cinder Market, Hassan does not buy effort. He buys results.
