Seth of Emberline Motors

Long before the roads emptied, Seth made a living deciding which vehicles were worth saving. He could look past a ruined finish, a seized wheel or a decade of neglect and see the machine that had once been underneath.

The collapse changed the condition of the inventory. It did not change the work.

Seth built Emberline Motors around the vehicles most survivors would have abandoned as impossible luxuries: imported sedans, high-performance coupes, armored trucks and rare models with replacement parts that no longer came from a warehouse. He recovered them one at a time, following rumors of locked garages, private collections and convoys that never reached their destinations.

He does not sell fantasies. Seth will tell a buyer exactly what a vehicle is, what it is not, and how painful it will be to replace once somebody wraps it around a tree outside Novodmitrovsk. The warning is free. The second vehicle is not.

The showroom only works because Seth knows when a machine is worth bringing home—and because Frida keeps the parts counter organized well enough to support everything he sells. He may be the face of Emberline, but even Seth checks with her before promising that a particular panel or wheel can be replaced.

Ordinary currency means little when the stock is this scarce. Seth deals in Zombie Notes, a price he considers appropriate for vehicles purchased with equal parts salvage work, stubbornness and danger.

At Emberline Motors, luxury is no longer about comfort. It is proof that something beautiful can still outrun the end of the world.
